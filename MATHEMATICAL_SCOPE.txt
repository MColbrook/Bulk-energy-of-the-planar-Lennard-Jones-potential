This document describes the analytic premises and finite program assertions

1. Imported analytic results
----------------------------
The source is OpenAI, An atomic certificate for triangular-lattice universal
optimality, 26 September 2026, at the commit in upstream/manifest.json.

Sections 3--5 provide the specific cardinal basis, Fourier formulas,
summable-coefficient convergence, the finite reference, and the unique exact
solution of all positive-node contact equations. In particular Proposition
5.1 gives, uniformly in the Gaussian parameter alpha >= 1, finite-reference
coefficient correction less than 10^(-5) and l1 tail less than 3*10^(-9).

Lemma 6.1 provides curvature envelopes. For two finite pole lists each of
norm at most a_L and two tail lists each of norm at most a_T, its bound for
one rescaled Fourier-paired input is

  |J''(s)|/2 <= a_L L(s0) + a_T T(s0),  s >= s0,

with L(14.5)=.00465, L(23)=.000023 and T=800 on these ranges.
The transformed slow constant column contributes at most .002 times the
absolute value of its coefficient for s >= 14.5. These envelopes already
account for the direct and transformed lists; no extra factor two is added.

Lemma 6.5 provides the sine-product lower bound

  P(s) > .054 (s-m)^2

away from its zeros, where m is a nearest zero. On [14.5,23] it provides
P(s)>3(s-m)^2 with m chosen from {16,19,21}. The joining segments stay in the
appropriate curvature ranges; for s>23 a nearest node is at least 25.

The upstream checker validates the finite arithmetic statements in that
source. The infinite-dimensional completion, spectral estimates, and their
analytic consequences follow from the cited results.

2. Additional analytic arguments in the manuscript
-------------------------------------------------
The manuscript proves the exact signed-mixture identity in the original
coefficient space. Uniform coefficient bounds and exponentially decreasing
normalization justify coefficient integration, Fourier transformation,
contact evaluation, and the required L1 limits.

The new exact residuals are

  J_R(s)=exp(pi*sigma*s) [G_+^flat(s)-U^flat(s)-Fourier(V)^flat(s)],
  J_T(s)=exp(pi*sigma*s) [G_-^flat(s)+Fourier(U)^flat(s)+V^flat(s)],

where b=sqrt(3)/2, s=b*r^2, sigma=17/50, and phi^flat(s) denotes the
squared-radius profile phi(sqrt(s/b),0) of a radial function on R^2.
The sign program uses h=sigma, H=tau=27/50 and side=0,1 for J_R,J_T.
Their values and first derivatives vanish at every selected positive node.
In code, value(s, side) evaluates the finite approximation to the residual.

Poisson summation and its dilation derivative, together with the normalized
zero-pressure identity, prove Fourier(H)(0)=0 exactly. The finite program
does not infer this exact zero from a numerically small interval.

The manuscript also supplies the Fourier lower bound for arbitrary finite
configurations, the physical rescaling, and the limiting upper bound from
triangular patches.

3. Exact lattice constants
--------------------------
src/lattice_constants.py encloses the unit-spacing triangular sums using

  Z_(2q)=6*zeta(q)*L(q,chi_-3), q=3,6,
  L(q,chi_-3)=3^(-q)[zeta(q,1/3)-zeta(q,2/3)].

It uses exact Fraction arithmetic and an explicit Bernoulli-polynomial
Euler--Maclaurin remainder. Its docstring derives that remainder. Decimal
arithmetic is used only for outward display, and the spacing enclosure is
checked by integer-power comparisons. The Epstein-zeta identity itself is
an analytic identity used by the computation.

4. Signed finite reference and correction budgets
-------------------------------------------------
src/reference_coefficients.py forms the original 20-by-20 sum/difference
systems and rigorously inverts them with Arb. It computes the Laplace
moments from upper incomplete gamma functions and combines the two input
sides exactly as required by the signed mixture. Target jets retained by
this reference are those at 1,3,4,7. Other contact targets belong to the
analytic correction; the finite reference need not interpolate them.

The program certifies

  M=integral_1^infinity (|w_+|+|w_-|)/D < .425,

and hence the imported Proposition 5.1 implies, for each new input,

  delta_f = 4.25e-6,    delta_t = 1.275e-9

as strict upper bounds for the finite and tail coefficient corrections.
The fixed columns have no correction. The signed-weight integrals use
w_+=A0*alpha^5-B0*alpha^2 and w_-=A0*alpha^(-6)-B0*alpha^(-3),
with 0<A0<B0. There is one zero of w_+ above 1, so the absolute mass is
computed by splitting exactly at alpha=(B0/A0)^(1/3).

src/verify_signed_moments.py independently reevaluates these scalar moments
from the computed A0,B0 intervals and proves that the fourth, sixth, and
eighth coordinates of the normalized signed target are negative. This
excludes the target from the original Gaussian parameter polytope, whose
corresponding coordinates are nonnegative. The sign inequalities for the
integrated auxiliary function are checked separately by verify_signs.py.

5. Finite spectral bounds and compact signs
-------------------------------------------
src/verify_signs.py expands the 15-factor sine product as a finite Fourier
sum with frequencies t_j=(j-15)/18. It computes the same expansions for
each finite cardinal. Direct and Fourier-transformed Gaussian atoms and
their derivatives are evaluated with Arb/Acb enclosures.

The rational tail cardinals admit common-frequency measures. Their total
variations are bounded by the two finite sums

  sum |p_j| pi |t_j|,
  (1/2) sum |p_j| pi^2 t_j^2,

both bounded by a common quantity less than 123. Fourier transformation
of slow atoms gives a prefactor below 4, rate magnitude below 14, and real
decay greater than 3/5 after multiplication by exp(pi*sigma*s). The program
checks the endpoints that imply these bounds for every frequency in
[-5/6,5/6], including the continuous measures of the tail. Consequently the
tail derivative-error bound for order j on s>=a is

  delta_t * TV * [(5*pi/6)^j + 4*14^j*exp(-3*a/5)].

The finite correction is bounded by delta_f times the sum of the largest
direct-column and transformed-column derivative envelopes. The two sides
have separate coefficient lists, both covered by these two summands.

On a rational interval [a,b], midpoint Taylor bounds enclose the complete
finite-reference function over every point of that interval. Intervals
are recursively bisected until a rigorous positive lower bound succeeds.
The algorithm has explicit depth limits and fails if it cannot certify a
whole interval. It never infers a sign from sampled values. Every accepted
leaf interval, its rational endpoints, and its certified lower bound are
recorded in signs.json. The endpoints are also checked to form a partition
of each region with no gaps or overlaps. The combined curvature and Taylor
covering is also checked for each profile.

Near a selected contact m, the code proves positive exact second derivative
on [m-1/16,m+1/16] after subtracting the correction envelope. Exact double
contacts then imply nonnegativity in the neighborhood.

Between contacts it uses either absolute-error control or a deleted-jet
bound. If F is the exact residual, R its full finite-reference residual,
and delta=F-R, the exact identity is

  F(s)=R(s)-R(m)-(s-m)R'(m)
       +delta(s)-delta(m)-(s-m)delta'(m).

The last three terms have absolute value at most

  (1/2) sup |delta''| (s-m)^2.

Both R(m) and R'(m) are explicitly subtracted. This is essential at nodes
whose target jets are omitted from the finite reference. No zero reference
jet is assumed there.

The compact check covers (0,14.5] in real space and [0,14.5] in Fourier
space. The special origin estimates cover (0,.1] and [0,.001], respectively.
The Fourier-origin argument uses the analytic identity J_T(0)=0 and the
verified lower bound J_T'(s)>.9396 throughout [0,.001].

6. Tail and all-radii conclusion
--------------------------------
The computed finite pole norms, with the correction added, are below .619.
The direct slow column of each residual is +gamma*P with

  .001436 < gamma < .001437.

The absolute half-curvature of the signed Gaussian target is less than
2e-17 for s>=14.5. Adding it to the imported curvature envelopes gives

  K_1=.619*.00465 +800*delta_t+.001437*.002+2e-17
     =.00288224400000002 < .001436*3 = .004308,

  K_2=.619*.000023+800*delta_t+.001437*.002+2e-17
     =.00001813100000002 < .001436*.054 = .000077544.

The first comparison applies on [14.5,23]; the second applies above 23.
Together with the exact double contacts and the cited sine-product lower bound,
they imply the signs on the entire remaining half-line. Thus the compact
and tail checks cover all required radii, conditional on the stated
analytic premises.

7. Scope of a successful run
----------------------------
A PASS means that the specified finite arithmetic assertions were satisfied
with the pinned dependency and verified source hashes. The analytic
interpolation, convergence, Fourier comparison, and thermodynamic-limit
arguments are supplied by the manuscript and its cited sources.
